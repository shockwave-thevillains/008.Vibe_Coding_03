The Pleistocene is the first epoch of the Quaternary Period. Its defining feature is the rhythm of **ice ages**: vast ice sheets grew across the northern hemisphere, melted back, and grew again, dozens of times over. Each time the ice thickened, sea level fell and shallow continental shelves became dry land.

## Early humans in island Southeast Asia

In 1891, Eugène Dubois found a skullcap at Trinil, on the banks of the Solo River in Java. "Java Man" became the first *Homo erectus* fossil ever found. The Sangiran site in Central Java has since yielded many more. A 2020 study that dated volcanic ash layers there concluded that *Homo erectus* most likely reached Java about 1.3 million years ago.

On Flores, Liang Bua cave holds the remains of *Homo floresiensis*, a small-bodied human announced in 2004. A 2016 re-study of the cave's layers placed the skeletal remains at roughly 100,000–60,000 years old.

## Our own species

Fossils from Jebel Irhoud in Morocco show that *Homo sapiens* was already around some 300,000 years ago in Africa. Groups of modern humans later spread into Asia, Australia, Europe and eventually the Americas, while other relatives such as the Neanderthals died out.

In South Sulawesi, a painting in Leang Karampuang cave, at least 51,200 years old, shows three human-like figures and a wild pig. Its discoverers describe it as the oldest known evidence of visual storytelling.

## The Sunda landmass

At the Last Glacial Maximum, around 26,000–19,000 years ago, sea level was about 120 m lower than today. Sumatra, Java, Borneo, Bali and the Malay Peninsula merged into a broad landmass known as **Sundaland**. The Pleistocene ends 11,700 years ago, as the climate warms and the Holocene begins.

*Note: The dates above follow the most recent publications cited, but dating in this field is revised often. This page is flagged for review.*
