The Cretaceous is the longest period of the Mesozoic, lasting almost 80 million years. The latest ICS chart places its start at **143.1 million years ago**; the older, often-quoted figure of about 145 million years has been revised.

## The world comes into flower

At the start of the Cretaceous, forests were still made of conifers, ferns and cycads. Then a newcomer arrived: **flowering plants**, or angiosperms. The oldest widely accepted flower fossils, such as *Montsechia* from Spain and *Archaefructus* from China, are roughly 130–125 million years old. By the end of the period, flowering plants and the insects that pollinated them had spread to almost every continent.

## Continents and oceans

Gondwana kept breaking apart. South America drifted away from Africa, and India began a fast journey north. With a warm climate and no permanent ice, sea level was very high, and shallow seas covered much of the land, including an inland seaway that split North America. The home-page globe shows a reconstruction for about 100 million years ago.

## The last day

About 66 million years ago, an asteroid roughly 10 km wide struck what is now Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, carving the Chicxulub crater, about 180 km across. Dust and aerosols blocked sunlight, food chains collapsed, and around three-quarters of species on Earth died out, including every dinosaur except the birds. The massive Deccan Traps eruptions in India, happening around the same time, are also studied as a contributing factor.
