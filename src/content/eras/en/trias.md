The Triassic is the first period of the Mesozoic Era, and it opens with a catastrophe. The end-Permian extinction was the largest die-off preserved in the rock record: most marine species vanished, and life on land took millions of years to recover.

## One enormous continent

Nearly all land was joined in **Pangaea**, a supercontinent stretching from pole to pole and surrounded by a single ocean, Panthalassa. Its interior lay so far from the sea that the climate was mostly hot and dry, with harsh seasons. The globe on the home page shows coastlines from a plate reconstruction for roughly 230 million years ago.

## The dinosaurs' opening act

Dinosaurs started out as just one reptile group among many; for much of the Triassic, crocodile relatives and other reptiles ruled the land. The oldest well-dated dinosaurs, such as *Eoraptor* and *Herrerasaurus*, come from Argentina's Ischigualasto Formation, in rocks about 231 million years old. Most were medium-sized animals that walked on two legs.

By the end of the period, pterosaurs were flying overhead and small mammal relatives lived in the undergrowth.

## A cracked ending

About 201.5 million years ago Pangaea began to rift apart. Colossal lava outpourings along the rift built the **Central Atlantic Magmatic Province**, and the gases they released very probably disrupted climate and ocean chemistry. Many reptile groups disappeared. The dinosaurs came through and inherited the world that followed.
