The Paleogene opens the Cenozoic Era, often called the age of mammals. It is divided into three epochs: the Paleocene, Eocene and Oligocene.

## After the disaster

Mammals had existed since the Triassic, but while dinosaurs ruled, most stayed small. After the end-Cretaceous extinction, empty ecological niches filled quickly. Within a few million years there were early horse ancestors, early primates, rodents and new kinds of predator. Birds, the only dinosaur line to survive, diversified too.

## A sudden heatwave

About 56 million years ago came the **Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum**. A huge release of carbon pushed global average temperature up by roughly 5–8 °C, acidified the oceans and reshuffled where plants and animals could live. Scientists now often study it as a point of comparison for modern climate change.

## Moving continents, changing climate

India collided with Asia around 50 million years ago and began lifting the Himalaya. On the shores of the shrinking Tethys Sea, whale ancestors such as *Pakicetus* still walked on land. Toward the end of the period, Australia and South America pulled away from Antarctica, ocean currents circling the South Pole strengthened, and around 33.9 million years ago large ice sheets began to cover Antarctica.
