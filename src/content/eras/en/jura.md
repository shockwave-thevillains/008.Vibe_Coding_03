The Jurassic is when dinosaurs truly took over the land. The climate was generally warm and humid, there were no permanent polar ice sheets, and forests of conifers, ferns and cycads spread widely.

## Continents drifting apart

The rift that opened at the end of the Triassic kept widening. The central Atlantic Ocean formed between North America and Africa, and Pangaea gradually broke into two great blocks: **Laurasia** in the north and **Gondwana** in the south. The home-page globe shows a reconstruction for about 170 million years ago, when the gap is already clear.

## The age of giants

Sauropods, plant-eating dinosaurs with long necks and tails, reached sizes no other land animal has matched. In North America's Morrison Formation, rocks about 154–153 million years old preserve *Brachiosaurus*, *Diplodocus* and their relatives, living alongside *Stegosaurus* and the predator *Allosaurus*.

## Feathers and wings

Around 150 million years ago, in tropical lagoons that became the Solnhofen limestone of southern Germany, lived *Archaeopteryx*. It had feathers and wings like a bird, but also teeth, clawed fingers and a long bony tail like a small predatory dinosaur. Later discoveries have made it clear that birds are part of the dinosaur family tree.
