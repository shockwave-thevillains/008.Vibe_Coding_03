The digital age overlaps with the 20th century and runs on to today. At its heart is one idea: any information, whether numbers, text, images or sound, can be turned into strings of 0s and 1s, then processed and sent by machines.

## From rooms to chips

**ENIAC**, one of the first general-purpose electronic computers, was completed in 1945 and shown to the public in February 1946. It filled a room and ran on thousands of vacuum tubes. In late 1947, Bell Labs scientists demonstrated the **transistor**, a small and more reliable electronic switch. Transistors were later packed together into integrated circuits, and the number that fit on a single chip kept doubling for decades.

## A network of networks

In October 1969, **ARPANET**, a research network funded by the US government, sent its first message between UCLA and the Stanford Research Institute. On 1 January 1983, ARPANET switched to TCP/IP, a shared language that let many networks join together into what we call the internet. In 1989, Tim Berners-Lee at CERN proposed the **World Wide Web**, and the first website went online in 1991.

## Indonesia in orbit

On 8 July 1976 (9 July in Indonesia), the **Palapa A1** satellite was launched. With it, Indonesia became the first developing country to run its own domestic communications satellite system, vital for linking telephone and television across an archipelago nation.

## The internet in your pocket

Mobile phones had existed since the 1970s, but the launch of the **iPhone** on 9 January 2007 popularised touchscreen phones with a full web browser. Within a decade, smartphones had become the main way many people around the world, including in Indonesia, went online.
