"The Middle Ages" is a term from European history: the period "in the middle", between the fall of the Western Roman Empire and the Renaissance. For much of the rest of the world, the same centuries were a golden age.

## The archipelago on the world's trade routes

From the 7th century, **Srivijaya**, centred around Palembang, controlled trade through the Strait of Malacca. The Kedukan Bukit inscription, dated 682 CE, is the oldest record linked to the kingdom, which was also known as a centre of Buddhist learning.

In Central Java, the Sailendra dynasty built **Borobudur** in the 8th and 9th centuries: the largest Buddhist temple in the world, with thousands of relief panels. Centuries later, in 1293, Raden Wijaya founded **Majapahit** in East Java. Under King Hayam Wuruk and his chief minister Gajah Mada in the mid-14th century, Majapahit's influence reached across much of the archipelago.

## Centres of learning

In Baghdad, under Caliph al-Ma'mun (813–833), the **House of Wisdom** became a hub of translation and research. Muslim, Christian and Jewish scholars translated and built on Greek, Persian and Indian work in mathematics, astronomy and medicine. The "Arabic" numerals we use today, which originated in India, reached Europe along this route.

## Plague and voyages

In 1347–1351, the plague later called the **Black Death** killed an estimated 30–50% of Europe's population and ravaged western Asia and North Africa. In the early 15th century, Ming dynasty fleets under Admiral **Zheng He** sailed seven times between 1405 and 1433, calling at Java, Sumatra, India, Arabia and the coast of East Africa.
