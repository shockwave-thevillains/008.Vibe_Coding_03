The Neogene has two epochs, the Miocene and the Pliocene. The continents already look much like a modern map, but the global climate slowly grows cooler and drier. The home-page globe shows a reconstruction for about 15 million years ago.

## A world of grass

As the climate dried, grasslands spread across several continents. Grazing animals with hard-wearing teeth, such as horses and antelopes, thrived in these open landscapes. Great apes were remarkably diverse across Africa, Europe and Asia during the Miocene, before many of their lineages died out.

## The human line

Fossils and genetic estimates suggest that the human lineage split from the chimpanzee lineage about 7 million years ago, in Africa. The oldest fossils from this time, such as *Sahelanthropus* from Chad, are rare and their interpretation is debated.

The picture gets clearer later on. About 4.4 million years ago, *Ardipithecus ramidus* could walk on two legs while remaining a capable climber. The Laetoli footprints in Tanzania, about 3.66 million years old, and the "Lucy" skeleton, about 3.2 million years old, show that *Australopithecus afarensis* walked upright as a matter of routine.

## Tools and a new genus

The oldest known stone tools, from the Lomekwi 3 site in Kenya, are about 3.3 million years old, older than any fossil of the genus *Homo*. A jaw from Ledi-Geraru in Ethiopia, about 2.8 million years old, is now regarded as the earliest evidence of *Homo*. The Neogene ends 2.58 million years ago, as the rhythm of ice ages intensifies.
