The 20th century was a century of acceleration. In a hundred years the world's population multiplied, cities grew fast, and new technologies, from household electricity to antibiotics, transformed daily life. It was also the deadliest century in the history of warfare.

## War and the atom

**World War I** (1914–1918) and **World War II** (1939–1945) were industrial wars fought on a global scale, killing tens of millions of people, including the victims of genocide. On 16 July 1945, Manhattan Project scientists detonated the first atomic bomb in the Trinity test in New Mexico. Weeks later, Hiroshima and Nagasaki were destroyed by atomic bombs. Atomic energy went on to power electricity grids and medicine, while nuclear arsenals hung over the Cold War.

## New nations

After World War II, a wave of decolonisation swept across Asia and Africa. On **17 August 1945**, Sukarno and Mohammad Hatta proclaimed Indonesia's independence; international recognition came only after four years of armed struggle and diplomacy. In April 1955, the **Asian-African Conference** in Bandung brought together delegates from twenty-nine countries and helped lay the foundations of the Non-Aligned Movement. Its archives are now listed in UNESCO's Memory of the World programme.

## Leaving the Earth

Rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union drove a space race. On 4 October 1957, **Sputnik 1** became the first artificial satellite. Twelve years later, on 20 July 1969, the crew of **Apollo 11** landed on the Moon. Photographs of Earth from space let many people see the planet, for the first time, as a single small and fragile whole.
