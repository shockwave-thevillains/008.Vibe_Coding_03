The Holocene, the geological epoch we live in, began 11,700 years ago as the climate warmed. Over the following millennia, people in several regions of the world, independently of one another, shifted from hunting and gathering to growing crops and keeping animals. This change is called the **Neolithic Revolution**, even though it unfolded gradually over thousands of years.

## Many places, many crops

In the **Fertile Crescent** of western Asia, wheat, barley and pulses were domesticated. In the **Yangtze** valley, rice was being cultivated by about 9,000 years ago. In south-western Mexico, maize was developed from the wild grass teosinte over a similar span. In the **New Guinea** highlands, the Kuk site shows wetland cultivation of taro and bananas from around 7,000 years ago.

## Monuments before fields

Göbekli Tepe in south-eastern Turkey changed how archaeologists think about this period. Its stone pillars, carved with animals, were raised around 9600–8700 BCE by people who still lived by hunting and gathering. A few thousand years later, Çatalhöyük in Anatolia had become a crowded farming settlement of houses packed wall to wall and entered through the roof.

## The Neolithic in island Southeast Asia

Neolithic culture reached the Indonesian archipelago later. In the Karama River valley of West Sulawesi, sites such as Minanga Sipakko hold red-slipped pottery, and rice phytoliths show that rice was farmed there at least 3,500 years ago. Many researchers link this spread to speakers of Austronesian languages, although the exact routes and timing are still being studied.
